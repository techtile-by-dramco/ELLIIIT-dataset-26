\section{Introduction}
\glsresetall

Large-scale experimental datasets have become increasingly important for wireless communication, sensing and positioning research because they decouple algorithm development from scarce hardware testbed time and expose downstream users to realistic synchronization errors, coverage gaps, and multimodal alignment constraints. Recent multimodal releases such as DeepSense~6G~\cite{alkhateeb2023deepsense} show how carefully documented measurement assets can accelerate communication-aware sensing research beyond their original experimental purpose by combining wireless measurements with external sensing streams. However, most public multimodal releases are not designed around synchronized ultrasonic propagation and distributed, receiver-side phase-coherent \gls{rf} channel measurements at the same indoor positions. In parallel, \gls{csi} fingerprinting and Wi-Fi positioning datasets have established important baselines for data-driven indoor localization~\cite{kazemi2022csifingerprint,dai2023wifi,kordi2024survey}, while massive MIMO and distributed MIMO channel-sounder efforts have shown the value of high-dimensional spatial \gls{rf} observations for fine-grained localization and machine learning~\cite{li2022massivemimo,euchner2022bigcsidata}. These datasets primarily address the \gls{rf} modality, and therefore do not expose acoustic waveforms that are temporally and spatially aligned with the channel observations.

Techtile was designed as a room-scale, distributed, synchronized infrastructure for reproducible experimentation in communication, sensing, positioning, and acoustic research~\cite{techtilePrimer,techtileOpen6g,techtile-acoustic}. Previous Techtile acoustic work and the released ultrasound dataset provide a reusable basis for ultrasonic positioning in the same room-scale environment~\cite{Echoes_of_accuracy,dataset_ultrasound_techtile}. ARFT differs from these earlier Techtile assets by co-locating an ultrasonic source and an \gls{rf} user antenna on the same rover and by recording one ground-truth position, one acoustic capture, and one distributed \gls{rf} snapshot for each acquisition cycle. The resulting release does not require later geometric matching between independent campaigns: the \gls{rf} and acoustic modalities share experiment and cycle identifiers, ground-truth coordinates, and a common processing workflow.

The distinguishing feature of ARFT is therefore not only that it contains multiple modalities, but that the modalities are aligned in a phase-coherent distributed testbed. In contrast to DeepSense~6G, ARFT targets room-scale indoor positioning with synchronized ultrasonic propagation and distributed sub-GHz \gls{rf} channel measurements rather than broader communication-aware sensing scenarios. In contrast to previous Techtile ultrasound data, ARFT adds co-located \gls{rf} measurements and cycle-level joins between \gls{rf}, acoustic, and position records. In contrast to pure \gls{csi} or massive-MIMO localization datasets, ARFT preserves raw acoustic waveforms together with the distributed \gls{rf} snapshot at the same rover stop. The receiver-side \gls{rf} infrastructure provides relative phase and amplitude across 42 ceiling antennas, which supports spatial fingerprinting and geometry-aware inference beyond single-link \gls{csi}. The acoustic side preserves raw ultrasonic chirp responses from 91 microphones, enabling ranging, fingerprinting, and multipath analysis. The common cycle-level synchronization, dense spatial scanning, open parsing scripts, and notebook-based processing pipeline make the dataset suitable for \gls{rf}-only, acoustic-only, and joint \gls{rf}-acoustic positioning studies.

ARFT packages one concrete synchronized measurement campaign in Techtile. It includes the acquisition stack, rover control, acoustic signalling, \gls{rf} measurement scripts, parsing code, processed data, and notebook-based analysis workflow used during the campaign. The release follows the broader open-science need for datasets that include data, code, and processing material rather than only final benchmark numbers~\cite{overview_datasets_indoor_positioning,dss6g}.

This paper documents the conducted experiments, elaborates on the resulting data, and explains the possibilities of the dataset for joint RF-acoustic positioning research.
